The status quo

What sponsors are tired of

The pharmacy benefit often fails in three places that matter more than the brochure discount: reports that cannot be checked, fees that hide inside drug cost, and member service that cannot explain a reject.

Reporting today

  • Quarterly PDFs with stored totals
  • Guarantee credits that never quite appear
  • Trend stories without claim-level proof
  • Finance and benefits see different numbers

Transparency today

  • “Transparency” as a slide, not a ledger
  • Pass-through sold, then exceptions
  • Or spread accepted because RFPs demand PEPM
  • No way to name every dollar either way

Service today

  • Phone trees and callback queues
  • Chatbots that cannot read the claim
  • Members escalate what should be one text
  • HR becomes the unofficial help desk

What Glass fixes

Recomputable reports, disclosed economics under any fee model, and service channels that answer from the same book as adjudication.

The offer

Three pillars

Everything else — pass-through vs PEPM, shared savings, clinical modules — sits under these three. If a feature does not make reporting clearer, economics more honest, or service faster, it is secondary.

1

Best-in-class reporting

Sponsor dashboards, guarantee reconciliation, rebate waterfalls, trend drivers, design scenarios, and a data agent for ad-hoc questions — all computed from claims, not carried forward from a PBM workbook. Finance can drill to the dollar.

2

Transparency with flexible pricing

Claim-level derivation and source citations stay mandatory. How Glass gets paid is a menu: pass-through, PEPM, shared savings, outcomes at risk, or disclosed margin — always as named lines, never as an unexplained plan-vs-pharmacy gap.

3

Excellent service by chat and text

Members get answers where they already are — chat and SMS — from an agent grounded in live eligibility, formulary, PA status, and claim history. Every figure comes from the book.

Reporting promise

No figure is a stored answer. If we cannot recompute it from claims and contract rules, it does not belong in a Glass report.

Pillar two

Transparency — without a single fee model

Transparency is the product layer. Pricing is packaging. Sponsors who need PEPM or shared savings still get claim-level derivation; they just see Glass revenue as an explicit line.

Always on

Transparency layer

  • Lesser-of arms on every claim
  • Source documents with citations
  • Settlement: invoice vs remit vs rebates
  • Guarantees from sponsor-visible records
Your choice

Commercial shell

  • Full pass-through
  • Transparent PEPM
  • Shared savings on verified interventions
  • Outcomes / SLA dollars at risk
  • Disclosed hybrid margin if required
Rule

Disclosure rule

If Glass earns money, it appears as a named fee, share, credit, or disclosed margin — never as an unexplained gap between what the plan pays and what the pharmacy receives.

Line for the room

“Buy the fee model that fits your RFP. You still get the ledger. You just cannot buy opacity.”

Commercial menu
